Check it by hand before you believe it

The MQH correlation estimates the upper-layer temperature rise in a ventilated compartment from the fire's power, the opening, the room's surface area and how fast the walls absorb heat. It is two lines of arithmetic and it catches the errors a simulation will not tell you about. The three flashover estimates below answer a different question: roughly how big a fire this room would need before it went to flashover.

MQH: ΔT = 6.85 [ Q² / (A_v√h_v · A_T h_k) ]^(1/3), with A_T the room's internal surface area less the opening, and h_k either √(kρc/t) while the wall is still heating through or k/δ once it has. Flashover estimates: Babrauskas 750 A_v√h_v, Thomas 7.8 A_T + 378 A_v√h_v, MQH 610 √(h_k A_T A_v√h_v). All four are from NUREG-1805, chapters 2 and 13. Lining properties are from its Table 2-3.

Watch out

The 6.85 coefficient was fitted to fires away from walls. A corner fire runs hotter, and the usual correction multiplies the temperature rise by 1.7. For the default values above that is the difference between about 324 °C and about 537 °C — so treat the pair as a band, not a number, and remember the correlation was fitted for rises up to about 600 °C.
